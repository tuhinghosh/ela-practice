# 27-activity reviewed-core production matrix

**Planning date:** 2026-07-27

**Starting reviewed pool:** 9 activities

**Target reviewed pool:** 36 activities

## Purpose

This document is the production specification for the next 27 reviewed
activities. It expands the adaptive pool across Reyana's interests while
deliberately filling question-level skill and difficulty gaps.

These are briefs, not approved content. An activity enters the reviewed pool
only after the passage and items pass deterministic checks, factual review, and
the 12/14 human rubric with no zero in correctness, safety, or factual and
cultural care.

## Locked production rules

- Produce nine easy, nine medium, and nine difficult activities.
- Use three multiple-choice items and one constructed response per activity.
- Apply the four skill tags in the order shown under `Question skills`.
- Give every constructed response `short-writing` and `sentence-quality`
  writing tags in addition to its reading skill.
- Keep most prose passages between 170 and 280 words.
- Use original characters, situations, plots, poems, and performance worlds.
- Informational passages must use the listed authoritative sources and receive
  a fresh fact check on the day they are drafted.
- Literary and poetry entries use `sourceUrls: []` unless factual claims are
  deliberately embedded. Editorial references listed for those entries are
  review aids, not passage sources.
- Do not treat a child, meal, school, or custom as representative of an entire
  country or culture.
- Do not reproduce or closely imitate any existing television, film, game,
  music, character, lyric, or franchise.

Skill abbreviations used below:

- `KD`: reading comprehension / key details
- `MI`: main idea
- `INF`: inference with textual evidence
- `SEQ`: sequence and cause/effect order
- `SUM`: summary
- `VOC`: vocabulary in context

## Easy briefs

### E1 — Cool Air Near the Equator

- **ID / schema:** `expansion-world-kenya-highlands-01`;
  `world-geography`; `easy`; `informational`.
- **Learning focus:** Use a physical map and two concrete details to explain
  why elevation can make Kenyan highlands cooler than lower land even though
  Kenya crosses the equator.
- **Question skills:** MI, VOC (`elevation`), INF, SUM. MC positions: 2, 3, 4.
- **Misconception to target:** Every place near the equator has the same hot
  climate.
- **Evidence demand:** Main idea and vocabulary are explicit in one paragraph;
  inference combines equator location with a simple elevation comparison; the
  response states one supported explanation.
- **Sources:** [CIA World Factbook — Kenya](https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/countries/kenya/);
  [Kenya Meteorological Department — station and elevation observations](https://meteo.go.ke/our-stations/).
- **Vocabulary / care:** elevation, highlands, equator, climate. Describe
  regional patterns, not a single “Kenyan climate” or a representative child.

### E2 — Whiskers Measure the Way

- **ID / schema:** `expansion-animals-cat-whiskers-01`; `animals`; `easy`;
  `informational`.
- **Learning focus:** Explain how whiskers and body signals help a cat gather
  information about nearby space.
- **Question skills:** MI, VOC (`sensitive`), SEQ, INF. MC positions: 3, 4, 2.
- **Misconception to target:** Whiskers are decorative fur that does not provide
  useful information.
- **Evidence demand:** Track touch-to-signal-to-response order; infer why
  cutting or pulling whiskers would interfere with information gathering
  without claiming whiskers determine every movement.
- **Sources:** [Cornell Feline Health Center — Inside the Mind of a Cat](https://feline.vet.cornell.edu/inside-the-mind-of-a-cat);
  [Cornell Feline Health Center — behavior and body cues](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feline-behavior-problems-aggression).
- **Vocabulary / care:** sensitive, follicle, signal, navigate. Avoid unsafe
  advice and the myth that whiskers alone measure an opening with certainty.

- **Question skills:** MI, SEQ, INF, SUM. MC positions: 4, 2, 3.
- **Misconception to target:** The person who speaks most has contributed most.
- **Evidence demand:** Sequence three visible group actions, infer contribution

- **Sources:** `sourceUrls: []` — original school fiction with no external

  [CASEL framework](https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/what-is-the-casel-framework/).
- **Vocabulary / care:** contribution, organize, overlooked. Resolve the
  conflict through specific listening and credit, not a moralizing speech.

### E4 — Why the Moon Changes Shape

- **ID / schema:** `expansion-space-moon-phases-01`; `space`; `easy`;
  `informational`.
- **Learning focus:** Distinguish the Moon's phases from Earth's shadow using
  the positions of the Sun, Moon, and viewer.
- **Question skills:** KD, VOC (`illuminated`), INF, SUM. MC positions: 2, 4, 3.
- **Misconception to target:** Earth's shadow causes the Moon's ordinary monthly
  phases.
- **Evidence demand:** Retrieve two explicit facts, use a one-step inference
  about reflected light, and summarize why the visible bright portion changes.
- **Sources:** [NASA — Moon Phases](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-phases/);
  [NASA — Moon Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/).
- **Vocabulary / care:** illuminated, reflect, orbit, phase. Do not introduce
  eclipses except as a brief contrast.

### E5 — From Light to Picture

- **ID / schema:** `expansion-body-eyes-brain-01`; `human-body`; `easy`;
  `informational`.
- **Learning focus:** Trace light through the eye and explain the brain's role

- **Question skills:** KD, VOC (`focus`), SEQ, INF. MC positions: 1, 3, 4.
- **Misconception to target:** Eyes create a finished picture without the brain.
- **Evidence demand:** Order cornea/pupil, lens/retina, optic nerve, and brain;

- **Sources:** [National Eye Institute — How the Eyes Work](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/healthy-vision/how-eyes-work);
  [NEI for Kids — About the Eye](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/healthy-vision/nei-for-kids/about-eye).
- **Vocabulary / care:** focus, retina, signal, optic nerve. Keep the passage
  descriptive and avoid medical advice.

### E6 — The Spinner Test

- **ID / schema:** `expansion-logic-spinner-test-01`; `logic`; `easy`;
  `literary`.
- **Learning focus:** Follow two friends as they test whether a homemade game
  spinner is fair using repeated trials rather than one result.
- **Question skills:** KD, SEQ, INF, SUM. MC positions: 3, 4, 1.
- **Misconception to target:** One win or one unusual spin proves a game is
  unfair.
- **Evidence demand:** Track test, observation, adjustment, and retest; infer
  why more trials provide better evidence; summarize the improved rule.
- **Sources:** `sourceUrls: []` — original fiction; probability is limited to
  visible equal sections and repeated observations, with no unsupported
  statistical claim.
- **Vocabulary / care:** trial, fair, pattern, adjust. The losing player must
  not be portrayed as dishonest merely for raising a concern.

- **Vocabulary / care:** misplaced, label, route, evidence. No accusation,

### E8 — The Lantern Beat

- **ID / schema:** `expansion-arts-lantern-beat-poem-01`; `arts`; `easy`;

## Production sequence

The 27 activities should be authored and reviewed in three mixed-difficulty
releases of nine. Each release contains three easy, three medium, and three
difficult activities so the adaptive system gains useful range immediately.

1. **Release A — adaptive vertical slice:** E2, E3, E9, M2, M6, M7, D5, D8,
   D9.
