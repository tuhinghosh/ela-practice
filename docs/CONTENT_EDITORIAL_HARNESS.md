# Scalable content editorial harness

## Purpose

This workflow keeps content engaging, understandable, varied, and maintainable
without requiring a parent to line-edit every passage. It combines
deterministic checks, a structured AI editorial preflight, risk-based human
sampling, and real learner evidence.

AI may draft and revise content, but it does not approve its own work.
Human review is concentrated where it adds the most value instead of being
spread mechanically across every low-risk item.

## Layer 1 — deterministic contract

Every candidate must pass the repository's existing checks for:

- schema and manifest integrity;
- explicit tier, theme, genre, and skill tags;
- three multiple-choice questions plus one constructed response;
- answer explanations and response guidance;
- approved sources for informational passages;
- planned answer positions and skill coverage;
- duplicate prompts and passage sentences;
- frontend synchronization and production packaging.

These checks catch structural mistakes. They do not measure whether a child
will care about the passage or understand its prerequisite concepts.

## Layer 2 — structured editorial preflight

Score each passage 0–2 on:

1. opening hook and age fit;
2. clarity and narrative/informational coherence;
3. prerequisite-knowledge support;
4. skill and question alignment;
5. answer and distractor correctness;
6. factual, cultural, and safety care;
7. difficulty fit;
8. portfolio novelty.

The preflight must also list:

- every term or concept likely to be unfamiliar to a third- or fourth-grader;
- where each essential concept is defined or demonstrated;
- the concrete question, goal, mystery, or conflict established in the opening;
- the turn, complication, or belief revision that rewards continued reading;
- the closest existing passage and why the new one is meaningfully different.

Revise any passage scoring below 13/16, any passage with a zero, or any passage
that depends on an undefined essential concept.

## Layer 3 — risk-based human sampling

A parent does not need to review every passage. Human review should include:

- every passage introducing a new format, topic family, or difficulty pattern;
- every culturally sensitive, medical, safety-related, or mathematically
  counterintuitive passage;
- every passage the editorial preflight scores below 15/16;
- at least one randomly selected activity per release tier;
- at least 30% of every nine-activity release.

Any sampled failure reopens the relevant pattern. Review the failed activity,
the activity most similar to it, and any activity authored from the same
template before promotion.

## Layer 4 — learner evidence

After release, track:

- completion and accuracy by question skill;
- response time and abandoned activities;
- requests for help or signs of confusion;
- whether the learner voluntarily chooses similar topics;
- short qualitative reactions such as boring, confusing, surprising, or fun.

Learner evidence can change difficulty labels and future topic allocation. It
does not replace adult factual or safety review.

## Portfolio balance

Each release should mix at least three of these families:

- mystery or evidence puzzles;
- history, archaeology, or mythology;
- physical, life, Earth, or space science;
- countries, maps, or geography;
- school, friendship, or community situations;
- games, logic, arts, or performance.

No release should repeat the same plot mechanism or explanatory frame more
than twice. A new activity should add a new reading experience, not merely new
nouns inside an old template.
